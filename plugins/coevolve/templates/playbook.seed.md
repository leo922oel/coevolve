# My Playbook

How I actually work with an AI coding assistant. Distillation rewrites the
data-derived sections below from real evidence; the human zone is mine.

**Core principle — keep verification ahead of generation.** Your real skill is
what you can verify minus what the AI can generate. Delegate generation freely;
never delegate judgment, understanding, or your mental model of the system.

## Ground rules (human zone — distillation never edits this)

Hand-written, not data-derived. Replace these with your own; they are the part
of the playbook that should change slowly and deliberately.

1. **Verification before generation.** Before implementing anything, write down
   what "correct" means — a test, an acceptance condition, an eval. Generated
   code has to clear that gate before it counts.
2. **Do not merge what you cannot explain.** If a session has outrun your
   ability to follow it, stop and cut the work smaller.
3. **Predict, then check.** Answer the design or debugging question yourself
   first, then ask. The gap between the two answers is the learning, and it is
   also how you learn when to trust the AI.

## Delegation split (human zone)

| Hand to the AI | Keep for yourself |
|---|---|
| boilerplate, syntax, API lookup, format conversion | defining what "correct" means |
| one-off scripts, test scaffolding, mechanical refactors | reading and being able to explain what ships |
| throwaway prototypes | architecture and trade-off decisions |
| draft docs and commit messages | hypothesis forming; the domain mental model |

## Keeping your own edge (human zone)

- **A deliberately manual zone.** Pick one area — your core domain, or a skill
  you want to grow — and do it without AI. Delegating every rep is how the
  ability quietly goes.
- **Extract the principle, not just the code.** When the AI solves something
  hard, write down *why* it works.
